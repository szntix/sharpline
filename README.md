# Sharpline

Fantasy football lineups, waivers, trades and player comparisons for you and your brothers. Runs on Netlify as an installable web app (PWA). Sign-in is a username and PIN; each account keeps its own leagues.

Version 2 replaces the sportsbook-props design. Props had to be fetched a day or more ahead to fit a free credit budget, so they were stale by game time. Everything now comes from free sources that need no API key and stay current through the week.

## Where the numbers come from

| Source | What it supplies | How it stays current |
|---|---|---|
| ESPN scoreboard | DraftKings spread and total (open and current), weather, game status | Fetched when the app asks, cached ~20 minutes |
| nflverse (GitHub releases) | Game logs, injury reports, closing lines for finished games, schedule | Updated by nflverse through the week |
| FantasyPros weekly consensus (via dynastyprocess) | Expert rankings, best/worst rank, start/sit grade | The file is checked against the week it belongs to (matched by kickoff times). If it is still last week's, it is withheld and shown as "not published yet" |
| Open-Meteo | Wind and temperature at kickoff | Forecast fetched per stadium |
| Sleeper | Rosters, leagues, projections | Live |

Every source carries a timestamp and a status (fresh, aging, waiting). The **data pulse** on the Week, Slate and Players screens and the Proof screen show them. Nothing stale is shown as if it were current.

The active week is the first week that still has an unfinished game, so Sunday and Monday night games keep the app on the right week.

## How a projection is built

For QB, RB, WR and TE: a stat model (recent form, plus the signals below) is blended with the expert consensus (and a share of Sleeper's projection). Weights per position came from the backtest. Kickers and defenses are estimated from the game line and are labeled "estimate"; they are not backtested.

### Backtest (research/backtest.py, 2021-2025 plus 2026 weeks 1-3)

Leave-one-season-out, with an ablation for each signal: a signal is kept only if removing it hurt in at least 4 of 6 tests and by at least 0.15% on average.

- Kept: Vegas team total (all positions), workload (targets, carries, target share; RB/WR/TE, helped 6 of 6), wind and cold (QB only), opponent defense rating (TE and QB, small effect).
- Left out: point spread, home field for RB/WR/TE, opponent rating for RB/WR. They did not help reliably.
- Experts beat a stats-only model nearly every season, so the app blends them. Blend weights (model/experts): QB .28/.72, RB .29/.71, WR .17/.83, TE .36/.64.
- One game is hard to predict (R^2 of roughly 0.09 to 0.37), so every projection is a range. Ranges are calibrated: the 10th-to-90th range contained the real score ~80% of the time.
- Head-to-head calibration is in coefs.js and drives the "X% to score more" figures on Compare.

Sportsbook props could not be backtested (no free historical archive), so they are not used.

## History capture, health checks and diagnostics

Some of what the app reads cannot be rebuilt later: how a betting line moved, when a ranking changed, who was listed Questionable on Friday. `capture-scheduled` wakes every 15 minutes, checks the calendar (Eastern time) and does nothing unless something is due.

| When | Lines, injuries, weather | Rankings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Within a game window (Thursday night, Sunday from 8:30 AM, Monday night, or within 2 hours before to 4 hours after any kickoff it has seen) | every 15 minutes | hourly |
| Other hours on Thursday, Sunday and Monday | hourly | hourly |
| Wednesday, Friday, Saturday | every 3 hours | every 3 hours |
| Tuesday | every 6 hours | every 6 hours |

Sleeper's add and drop trends are read once a day after 6 AM. Only changes are stored: a row is added when a game's line (or a player's rank, status or forecast) differs from its last row. One small document per week and kind lives in the `capture` Blobs store (`lib/capture.mjs` explains the layout). `/api/capture` shows what is saved and when each kind last ran; `/api/capture?kind=lines&season=2026&week=4` returns the rows.

**Health checks.** `lib/shape.mjs` checks the format of every source (the field names ESPN, FantasyPros, nflverse, Sleeper and Open-Meteo return). A source that still answers but changed its format gets a red dot and a plain note in the freshness pill, instead of silently showing nothing. The capture job saves the verdicts as `health` rows.

**Diagnostics.** `/api/diag` walks the same steps the app does for kickers and defenses (player list, game, line, projection) and states where each drops out, with counts. Open it when a tab looks empty.

**Kickers and defenses.** Defenses are matched to expert ranks by team abbreviation (Sleeper's id for a defense), and kickers the id table lacks are matched by name and team. On the Players screen the K and DST tabs always list everyone and say why a row has no number.

## Design

A quiet neutral frame with turf green as the brand, and color only where it carries meaning.

- **Who:** six position colors (QB red-orange, RB light green, WR deep blue, TE yellow, K violet, DST brown), chosen so the worst pair stays 15 or more apart under every color-vision type, and every chip carries its letters. Each team has a color and a logo plate; the logo sits over the team letters, and if it fails to load the letters remain.
- **How likely:** one red-to-green scale for every chance (win chance, chance of a big game, percentiles). Red is darker than green so the two stay apart for red-green color-blind viewers, and the number is always printed beside the shade. Amounts with no good or bad stay neutral.
- **Frame:** soft neutrals in light and dark, 1 px outlines, a faint shadow in light mode. A stronger edge is kept only where a boundary is what identifies a control or a selected state. Selection is a raised pill, and position bars take that position's color.
- **Sizes:** nothing under 12 px, controls 44 px tall (segments at least 40 px wide), and 7 to 8 player rows fit on a 390 by 844 phone.
- **Rows:** every row is a solid slate color and exactly two lines, so nothing is taller than anything else. The team shows only as a stripe on the left edge, beside its logo plate. Injury status is the ring chip beside the name; any other condition is at most two short tags on the right of the second line (Shootout, Model +4.5, Running hot). The full sentence for each tag is on the player page and is read out to screen readers.
- **Slate bars:** each side's bar is its team's color (chosen to stay visible in light and dark) and splits the game's expected points between the two teams. Both team columns are a fixed width, so every bar starts and ends in the same place.
- **Side by side (Compare):** in each row the better value is a solid bar in that player's team color and fills its half; the other is an outline at its true proportion. For expert rank, lower is better, so the bar grows as the rank number shrinks. If either side has no number, the row shows both values and no bars.
- **Team color on rows:** off by default (plain slate rows with a team stripe). Leagues, Account and data, has a switch at the bottom to tint each row and the player card with the team's color; the choice is remembered on the device.
- **Dropdowns:** Sort and Show act only when an option is chosen (`data-change`), never on the tap that opens them, so opening and dismissing a dropdown changes nothing.
- **Version:** the build number shows in Leagues, Account and data, on any error screen, and at `/version.json`, so it is always clear which build is live.
- **Players sort:** Blended (the projection we show), Our model (the stat model alone, with its own number on each row; players it does not cover come last), Expert rank, and We like more. A one-line note under the bar says what the order means, using the real weights for the position you are on.
- **Moves:** one 12 px rhythm down the page. Every list row has a fixed number column, so numbers, gain tags and buttons line up from row to row. Waiver rows show the gain in the action row, trade ideas show Give and Get on separate lines, and Hot and cold rows use the same plate, name and number as everywhere else.
- **Compare:** one shared "score at least X" line across both players. Tap 10+, 15+ and so on, or drag the line on either plot; both plots, both captions and the chips move together, and the line is remembered for that pair. On a phone the "who scores more" verdict sits in a strip under the two team panels so each team gets half the width and long names wrap instead of clipping.
- **Team logos without a pale disc:** the logo is drawn as a one-color silhouette using CSS filters on the same image. Compare and Game panels carry a large soft watermark that always pushes the panel away from its text color (dark mark under white text, light mark under dark text), so it can only raise contrast. The Player card has a faint corner mark. Small team-color badges above the Compare plots show the logo in white (or black) on the team color. If a logo cannot load, nothing is left behind and the team letters show instead.
- **Readable text on every team color:** text on a team-color plate or panel is held to 4.5:1 for all 32 teams. Two mid-tone brand colors (Carolina and the Chargers) are nudged by about 7 of 441 RGB units for this; every team's stripe keeps its exact brand color. The Player card uses a lighter team tint (6%) than list rows (12%) because it also carries the watermark.
- **Trades:** every player in trade ideas, in the You give and You get chips, and in the menus carries a colored position chip. Reviewing a trade shows "Your roster after the trade" (or the other team's, with a toggle): starters in slot order, bench, and who is leaving, with New, Moves up and Moves to bench tags and a plain reason when an incoming player is not cracking the lineup ("Behind Henry by 2.2"). It uses the same lineup optimizer and the same per-game rest-of-season values as the verdict above it, so the numbers cannot disagree.
- **Position tabs:** ALL, QB, RB, WR, TE, FLEX, K, DST are all written in capitals. A selected single position fills with its color; a selected ALL or FLEX (a group) is the same neutral pill, with no gradient strip. The FLEX slot chip on lineups uses the same capitals.
- **Editing a league:** Edit scrolls straight to the editor and turns into Close, so a tap always shows a result even with several leagues. Manual leagues also have Edit roster (jumps to the roster panel) and an Edit roster link under the bench on Week. The editor has a jump row (My roster, Starting lineup, Scoring, Other teams) and a Done button at the top. Every control in the editor, including each remove button, has a touch area of at least 44 px, and removing a player says who was removed.
- **Trade ideas never go silent:** a suggestion must improve your lineup by more than 1 point over the season and theirs by more than 0.5, at similar value, across two positions, so it needs a position where you hold more than your lineup uses. When there are none, the screen says so with a count of where each possible swap fell out (same position, would not improve yours, improves yours but not theirs, too lopsided), and "Worth a pitch" lists up to five deals that help you but not them, labeled "they lose" or "they break even". In a league with no other teams (a custom league), it says trade ideas need them and offers Add other teams. The suggestions themselves are unchanged from the original build (checked on 41 leagues).
- **Opening screen:** a turf field with the logo's yard lines and ball, matching the phone's own launch screen (manifest background is turf green), held until the app draws its first screen, with a 12-second safety timer.
- **Trade values:** a player at or below replacement level reads "depth" and a player with no projection reads "no projection", instead of a bare 0. Dynasty and keeper leagues show ages in the trade builder, and the league editor explains each league type. The aging curve now keeps declining past its last listed age (it used to go flat, which over-valued players over 30 in dynasty).
- **Trade value counts depth:** when the league's rosters are known, value is points above the best free agent actually available at that position (never above the old starting-lineup estimate), so bench depth that beats the waiver wire has a number.
- **Suggestions follow the calculator:** with a player only in You get, the list becomes "Ways to get him" (each of your players for him, best for your lineup first); with a player only in You give, "What he could bring back". A free agent gets "add him without a trade". Review loads the full swap.
- **Projections from:** one control, in Settings (Blended, Our model, Experts, Sleeper), drives every number and lineup in the app; Blended is the tested default. When it is not Blended, a strip under the header on every screen says so, with Use Blended and Settings. Week has no switch; it shows the four sources' lineups side by side where they disagree and what each alternative gives up by the Blended numbers. A player a source does not cover keeps his Blended number.
- **Role check:** the stat model reads past usage and cannot see who is starting now. When the experts project a player at less than half of the model's number (typically a backup whose history comes from old starts), the model sits him out that week: the blend uses the experts, Our model keeps his Blended number, and his page says why.
- **Availability:** on the official injury report, Questionable counts 80% and Doubtful 0% (tested: about 1% less projection error than the earlier rule, roughly half a point a week). A Sleeper-only status keeps the earlier rule (Questionable 100%, Doubtful 25%), and within 90 minutes of kickoff a Questionable player who has not been ruled out counts as playing.
- **Advanced stats:** from the nflverse weekly file (EPA per dropback, carry and target, CPOE, WOPR, air-yards share, depth of target, yards after catch, catch rate, first downs). The Player page shows them with position percentiles, Compare adds them as rows, and the Game page shows each defense against each position.
- **Compare bars:** each bar is the value against this week's best at the position (a full bar is the leader), with a tick for a typical starter; solid still marks the better of the two.
- **Dropdowns and fields:** every field and dropdown is 16 px (smaller text makes iPhones zoom the page on focus), 44 px tall, labeled, and drawn with the same arrow. Sort and Show are a label with an invisible native picker over it, so the arrow sits right after the text.
- **"You get" and "You give" suggestions:** a swap is kept only if the two sides are within about 30% in value (or 15 points for small values), or it improves both lineups at a price within a factor of two. Each row says which.
- **Good, neutral, bad at a glance:** five tones (strong, good, neutral, weak, bad) used the same way everywhere, each with a fill and a text color, always with words or arrows too. Player efficiency tiles show a meter filled to the player's percentile at his position, a one-word grade (Elite, Strong, Average, Weak, Poor), and the number in the grade's color; a negative EPA or CPOE can never be green. Depth of target is shown as Deep, Mixed or Short without a good or bad color. The Game page's defense table is colored from the point of view of the player facing the defense (green soft, red tough) with an arrow and a signed percentage. A trade idea's "for you" number is colored by size.
- **Folds:** Things to know and Lineup by source are folded until tapped, with a count; each fold remembers whether it is open.
- **Worth a pitch:** only deals worth considering (close in value, a gain of at least 3 points for you, and they lose no more than 5), at most three, and never more than six cards in total with the win-win ideas, which always come first.
- **One source, everywhere:** when the projection source is not Blended, every derived number follows it: a player's spread (bad week, great week, the dots) and every win probability are recomputed from that source's number with the same formula the projection uses, the Player page says whose number is on top and shows the Blended one for reference, and the rank chip is named for the source. Compare's headline always names the favorite, with the same percentage as the sentence below it; if the favorite projects lower but wins more often, the sentence says so.
- **Live record (Proof):** every three hours until kickoff the app saves each player's inputs and the numbers it computed from them; a team's rows freeze when its game starts. Grading uses the saved numbers, never today's model, so changing the model cannot rewrite a past week (rows saved before this was added are re-scored with the current model and counted on the screen). The four sources are compared on exactly the same player-weeks (players who played, with all four numbers); the app's own number for everyone is reported separately. A second table shows who actually played, by designation (Healthy, Questionable, Doubtful), next to what the app assumed, which checks the Questionable discount every week. The screen also says how long before kickoff the numbers were saved (up to three hours) and counts no-shows. The 80% range check counts players who played.
- **Matchup and game meter:** lineup rows and lists carry no text tags and no source caption. Under the projection there is one wordless five-bar meter for the matchup and game: the stat model's own estimate, in points, of what this week's setting adds (the betting market's team total, the opponent's defense against his position, wind and cold, home field; workload is left out), ranked within his position this week (85+ great, 65+ good, 35-65 neutral, 15-35 tough, under 15 very tough). Players the model does not cover (kickers, defenses, role-unclear backups) get none. A caption remains only for exceptions: an estimate, a bye, a number kept from Blended. The health ring (Out, D, Q, DNP) is unchanged. The profile has a one-line version in words. Bar fills use the tone's text color so they hold 3:1 against the card in both themes.
- **Compare background:** only stats that apply to both positions are shown (a QB pair never shows WOPR or targets; a WR pair never shows carries); a Matchup & game row; one line giving the stat model's two reasons the players differ (recent form and workload, matchup and game); and one line on whether our model, the experts and Sleeper agree (the Blend is not counted, it is built from them).
- **When data updates, and what is idle (decided):**
  - **Season awareness.** The two scheduled jobs (snapshots every 3 hours, line and injury capture every 15 minutes) first read the regular-season calendar (nflverse, cached 12 hours) and do nothing unless a game is within 7 days ahead or 3 days behind. That is about 130 days a year; the rest of the year they make no requests beyond that one cached calendar check. If the calendar cannot be read they assume it is in season, because a missed week costs more than a wasted pull.
  - **In season, capture frequency by phase:** game window (near a kickoff, or the usual Thursday night, Sunday and Monday night) every 15 minutes, other game days hourly, Tuesday every 6 hours, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday every 3 hours. Only changes are saved.
  - **Big files are revalidated, not re-downloaded.** When the stats file's cache lifetime ends the app asks nflverse whether it changed (ETag): unchanged costs a few hundred bytes and no parsing (an unchanged 8 MB file checked hourly is one download a day instead of 24). A failed check keeps the data already held.
  - **A new season** starts before its stats file exists. A missing current-season file means "no games yet", not an error, and is not remembered, so it is asked for again. Last season's file supplies the form players start the year with (kept 30 days, revalidated). Last season's saved snapshots stay in storage and are never graded again; the live record only grades the current season.
  - **Cache lifetimes:** stats 50 min, usage 60 min, expert rankings 45 min, injuries 30 min, Sleeper projections 30 min, Sleeper player list 24 hours (Sleeper asks for at most once a day), ID table 3 days, schedule and calendar 12 hours, weather 3 hours, outlook 6 hours.
  - **Phones** check the feed every minute while a game is live, every 3 minutes near a kickoff, every 10 minutes the rest of the week, every 30 minutes if no games are listed, and every 3 hours when games are listed but none is within a week. A first or failed load retries in 10 minutes.
  - **Many users share one copy.** Feed (60 s), usage (5 min), accuracy (5 min), players (1 h) and outlook (1 h) are shared at the edge and in storage, so a phone opening the app does not start a pull. Only sign-in, profiles and the Sleeper roster proxy (2 minute cache) are per user. Known limit: if many phones hit an expired cache at the same instant they can each ask the source once; with revalidation that is a few hundred bytes each unless the file really changed.
- **Stat rankings:** every efficiency tile on a player's page is a link. It opens everyone at his position ranked by that stat (best first), with him highlighted and scrolled into view and a line such as "#7 of 32 QBs", with ties shown as T-7 and the next rank skipping ahead. The tile says the rank in words ("#14 of 80 TEs") with a meter for the percentile. A player is ranked on a stat only with at least 2 games and the NFL's own leaderboard pace per week of the season: 14 dropbacks for quarterbacks, 6.25 carries for rushing, about 3 targets (the NFL's 1.875 catches) for receiving, 8 touches for a back's first downs, so a backup's few snaps cannot lead a list while every starter clears the bar easily. Players below it are not ranked but are still listed in a "Not ranked" fold with the reason ("1 game" or "7.5 dropbacks a week"), their tile says why instead of showing a grade, and their page says where the number would sit among the ranked players. Season volume totals (dropbacks including sacks, carries, targets, receptions) come from the same nflverse file; data saved before they existed falls back to the games rule alone. Filters: Everyone, Free agents (leagues with the other teams entered), My team; ranks stay global when filtered. Depth of target ranks deepest first with no good or bad coloring. The tile and the page share one definition (views/advstats.js) so they cannot disagree.
- **Settings:** the active league always shows; the other leagues fold under "Other leagues" with a count, and the fold remembers whether it is open.
- **Line movement on the Slate:** a total that moved 1.5 points or more since the line opened shows an arrow and the amount next to the total (red down, green up), with a screen-reader sentence and a one-line legend.
- **Injuries:** both injury areas from the report show (for example "Knee, Hamstring"), the game report first, else the practice report; repeats are dropped.
- **Fix:** the Blend bar on a player's page always shows the true Blend, whichever source is selected (it showed the selected source's number).
- **Trade board:** Trade ideas are the best distinct ideas across one-for-one swaps and packages of up to two players each way (two for one, one for two, two for two), found one team at a time so the screen stays responsive (a "Checking..." line shows while it works). Every idea improves both lineups (more than 1 point for you and 0.5 for them over the rest of the season), at a fair price (one-for-ones within 0.70 to 1.45 of each other in value, packages 0.75 to 1.35), and a package must be minimal: taking either player out of it makes it stop working, so nothing is a throw-in. Only the top nine players by value on each side are considered, never kickers or defenses, and same-position one-for-ones are skipped. Ideas are ranked by your gain plus half of theirs (simpler trades first on a tie), then thinned so one of your players is offered at most twice, a target once, one manager at most three times, and no variant of an idea is shown twice; at most eight are shown. Each card has a type label, a plain line ("You: TE +5.1 · They: RB +2.3 a week", the two biggest lineup slot changes on each side), and tags: "Fills a weak spot", "Sell-high: Name" (a player of yours on the Hot list) and "Buy-low: Name" (a player you would get on the Cold list), the same top-8 lists as the Hot and cold tab. Nothing predicts whether a manager will accept.
- **Waiver backfill:** lineups in trade math fill any slot nobody on the roster can fill, or where the best rostered player is below waiver level, with the best free agent at that position (the league's waiver level, or the starting-lineup estimate when the other teams are not entered). Giving up depth or a starter is scored against what you could really do instead of against zero. The "roster after the trade" preview uses the same lineups and shows a "Free agent" row for such a slot. Kickers have no waiver level and stay empty.
- **Focused suggestions** (a player in You get or You give) also offer a pair of players from the other side when no single player matches in value; a pair is only shown when neither player alone would already be a realistic offer.
- **Fresh visits start open:** arriving at Trades from another screen, or from Waivers or Hot and cold, starts on Anyone with an empty calculator; staying on the screen (redraws, data refreshes) keeps a trade you loaded.
- **Shared data stays current while the app is open:** the player table (statuses), game logs, schedule and accuracy are re-read every two hours while the app is visible (and on return if older), quietly, redrawing only if something changed. An official Questionable or Doubtful game designation outranks a stale Sleeper IR, PUP, NA, suspended or COVID tag (practice participation alone does not).
- **Row heights:** the caption under a number and the matchup meter take the same 18 px, so list rows are the same height whether or not a meter shows.
- **Teams:** Slate has a Games | Teams toggle. Teams ranks all 32 teams by a rating (points better than average on a neutral field) that blends results adjusted for opponents with what the betting lines say, with week-over-week arrows. Each team has a page built like the player header: record, rating and trend, this week's game with win odds taken from the betting line (never a home-made forecast), pass and run efficiency on both sides with ranks of 32, pass rate and pace, margins by game, who gets the targets and carries, and a schedule ribbon colored by how soft the opposing defense has been, with your league's playoff weeks outlined and later weeks dimmed. Served by `/api/teams`, built from the schedule file and the weekly stats rows the app already reads, cached about 15 minutes, loaded only when a team screen opens and refreshed with the other shared data after that. A 3,408-game backtest (2010 to 2025) found the closing line beat every rating built from results or lines, so the page says so and shows the numbers.
- **Weekly progression:** the server's current week moves to the next one as soon as every game of the week is final; a live, postponed or unplayed game holds it; week 18 stays 18; with no games on the scoreboard it shows week 1; with ESPN down it uses Sleeper's week and says so. An open app on the current week asks the server which week is current, moves on by itself and says "Week N is here"; one browsing another week is never pulled away but keeps the "current week" banner right. The feed reports `weekInfo` (ESPN's week, our week, games final of total) for checking a change afterward.
- **No team, no projection:** a player with no team is projected 0, shown as "No team", left out of lineups while anyone else can fill the spot, and given no trade or rest-of-season value until he signs.
- **Team color setting:** "Team color on player rows" tints player rows only. Profile headers, team rows and Slate games are always colored.
- **Lineup by source** compares who each source starts, not which slot: the same players in different slots is "same", and a player only one source starts or only one source sits is bolded and named.
- **Late flex:** on the Week screen the flex-type slots go to the players whose games kick off latest, so a late swap stays possible. That never changes the total. A bench player may also take a flex spot as a tie, only when his projection is within 0.5 points, nobody involved is hurt, his game is at least 3 hours later, and the total given up stays under 1.0. With no kickoff time known, nothing moves. A note under Starters says what moved. Flex slot chips wear the color of the position playing them.
- **Teams on the player page:** a strip under the header (his team's city, record, rating rank, and its style in words, linking to the team), and three sections after everything else: Role over time (his share of the team's targets, or carries for a back, game by game, from the weekly team totals), Who shares the targets or carries (the team's top four with him outlined, added as a fifth entry if he is outside them), and Next weeks (five weeks of opponents colored by how well they defend his kind of play: pass defense for receivers and quarterbacks, run defense for backs; context only, never a projection input). Quarterbacks get the strip and Next weeks; team defenses and players with no team get none. The game page's large team abbreviations link to the team pages. If the team data cannot load or comes back unusable, the page is exactly as it was.
- **One page skeleton:** player, team and game pages answer the same questions in the same order: header, right now, why, people, ahead, reference. On the player page "Worth knowing" now follows the big-game odds, the "why" sections stay together (usage, how we got the number, where he stands, experts, the matchup) and Recent games closes them before Role over time.
- **Game page** (top to bottom): the header, total and spread as before; Win odds (from the posted spread, never a home-made forecast; only for games not yet played); What to know (the existing notes plus rank notes, which appear only for a top-five or bottom-five defense or both offenses at a pace extreme, and only once both teams have two games); Line movement; The matchups (each offense against the other defense, pass and run, as facing rank bars; an edge is named at a gap of 10 or more ranks); Why the total is what it is (each team's average combined points against the posted total, plus pass rate and pace in words); Fantasy players in this game; Where the volume goes (target and carry shares for both teams, tagged Out, Doubtful or Questionable from the same status the lineup uses); and the defense-versus-position table with each cell's rank of 32 added. Without team data the page shows exactly what it always had, in the new order.
- **Matchup card and score bug:** with an opponent chosen, the Week screen opens on one card that is itself a 100-yard field: the grass bands, the yard lines, the yard numbers and the ball all sit on the same 10% grid (the old card drew them on three different grids, so they drifted apart by about 16px). It shows the win chance, "to beat" and the opponent's name, how many points you are from a coin flip and what each point is worth (from the two projections and their likely ranges), the bar shaded by the chance with the ball at that many yards, both projected scores and ranges, and chips for the earliest kickoff among your starters and for any starter who is flagged (one is named with his status, several become a count; the chip jumps to the Starters list). Once the card has scrolled up under the header, a slim copy (your score, the chance, the opponent's short label and score, the field, the kickoff and alert text) slides in below the header; tapping it returns to the top. The opponent's short label ignores filler words ("The", "Mr", "Team", "Los"), becomes initials for three or more real words ("Smokin' Jay Cutler" is SJC), is otherwise the first real word, and is abbreviated with an ellipsis past ten characters. Long names wrap in the card. All turf cards use bands and lines that repeat every 10%.
- **Bench:** players who cannot play (out, IR, suspended, no team, bye, or an official Doubtful that projects 0) sit at the bottom under "Not playing this week"; everyone else stays in projection order.
- **Streaming suggestions now look at the next weeks, not just this one.** Before, the board ranked by this week's projection alone and the look-ahead chips were only decoration. Now each defense and kicker is scored by what it adds over a normal streaming pickup (the average of the best three available) in this week and the next two, weighted 1, 0.5 and 0.3 for defenses (1, 0.4 and 0.2 for kickers), and may be held one, two or three weeks (`js/plan.js`). Judging by the gain over a normal pickup makes two good weeks in a row beat three decent ones; a bye scores zero so a defense cannot be held through one (a defense with a bye next week is worth only this week); and this week stays in charge. The board shows a recommended move (Add, or Keep your current one when it is within the 0.4-point cost of a pickup of the best), "Other ways to play it" (most points now, best two-week run, best three-week hold, each only when it differs), and tags on every row (Hold 2 weeks, Bye in W7, Game already started). This week always comes from the lineup's own projection, so the board works even when the team table has not loaded; a game that has started counts as zero. Replaying five seasons of streaming with random waiver pools (`research/plan_sim*.py`): the plan beat always taking the top score by about 0.09 points a week net of pickups (about 0.02 either way), while giving up only about 0.01 points of this week's expectation. It changes the number-one pick only about 3% of the time and the top five about 6%, because a clear edge this week should win; the variety is in the kind of move (about half of all top-five lists contain a defense with a bye in the next two weeks, and a hold of two weeks or more is the best move about 14% of the time).
- **Defense and kicker profiles now carry the team profile, and the Streaming schedule starts at this week.** The Players page for a defense used to switch the team strip off and show none of the schedule; it now shows the team strip, "How this defense has played" (points allowed, overall adjusted, pass and run defense, each ranked out of 32) and "Schedule ahead" from this week through week 18, each chip rated by `M #`, the matchup's rank among all defenses playing that week (this week from the betting line, the same number as the lineup projection; later weeks rough, from the opponent's scoring and this defense's points allowed). A kicker's page shows "His offense" (points a game, passing and rushing offense ranks, this week's weather or roof) and a Schedule ahead with the home stadium's roof for every week. The Streaming ribbon uses the same builder (`defenseWeeks` and `scheduleSection` in `js/views/teams.js`), so the board, the defense page and the team page cannot disagree, and it lists this week plus the next three (W5 vs PHI, W6 vs HOU, W7 bye, W8 vs IND). The schedule parser now keeps each game's stadium roof (as text) into the team payload; the weather and roof helper moved to `js/conditions.js`.
- **Offseason fallback for Teams.** Between the league year turning over and the new schedule being published, the team table keeps showing last season's final table with a banner ("The 2027 schedule is not out yet") and switches by itself once the schedule exists, instead of showing an empty list. Checked by replaying the season shifted forward a year (`research`-style replay in the test suite): the week flip, week 18, pre-season with no new stats, and offseason with no new schedule.
- **Streaming matchups, corrected and checked against the real schedule.** The next-weeks chips listed the next three *games* and tacked any bye on at the end (Jacksonville read HOU, IND, @BAL, bye when the bye is week 7). They now show the next three *weeks* in order with the bye in its own week and week numbers on every chip (W6 HOU, W7 bye, W8 IND), from `js/lookahead.js`. Each defense row now also says who it plays this week, home or away, the kickoff, and what the line says that offense will score (previously only the team code). Checked against the 2026 schedule: all 32 teams for every week 1 to 18 (576 cases), the Jacksonville, Houston and Cincinnati rows, and for all 30 defenses playing in week 5 both the opponent and the opponent's expected score against the real lines. The ranking and the stream-or-hold gain use only this week's line, so the look-ahead chips never changed them. The chips' coloring is still the rough measure; testing showed a blend that also uses how many defense points an opponent's offense has given up predicts slightly better (about 5.2% against 3.9% better than a flat average, week 5 on, 2021 to 2025), which would need a server addition and is not built yet.
- **The opponent now follows the week.** Before this, the opponent was one saved value, looked up from Sleeper only during a roster sync that runs at most every 10 minutes. When the app moved to a new week it kept showing last week's opponent, with a win chance computed against the wrong team, until that sync ran; if Sleeper did not answer at that moment, the old opponent stayed all week; a bye or an eliminated team kept the old opponent; and a manual league never changed. Now each week has its own opponent (`opps`, saved with the profile): the week's matchup is looked up from Sleeper the moment that week is on screen, quietly retried every 15 seconds until Sleeper answers, and never shows last week's opponent in the meantime. A bye or an unpaired team clears the opponent and says why. A manual league clears at the new week and says "Last week you played X. Pick this week's opponent..."; a pick counts for its own week only. Browsing back to an earlier week shows that week's opponent without another lookup.
- **Defense and kicker projections recalibrated on five seasons of results.** Checking the old hand-set numbers against 2021 to 2025 (2,238 defense games, 2,232 kicker games) showed they were off: defenses projected 7.2 points against 6.2 actual and were too steep in the opponent's expected score (calibration slope 0.74), and kickers projected worse than simply using the league average. The components are refitted (defensive touchdowns, for example, were assumed at 0.16 a game and are 0.07) and still counts that your league's own scoring weights turn into points. Held-out seasons: defenses 6.13 against 6.20 actual, kickers 8.17 against 8.16. Kickers now also use the spread and the forecast (wind, cold, a roof). Where Sleeper has its own kicker projection the two are averaged 50/50, because only ours has been checked against history. The JavaScript is checked against an independent Python implementation on all 4,470 games (largest difference 0.002 points). Sources: `research/` plus `js/model.js`.
- **Streaming tab in Moves** (fourth tab): this week's best defenses and kickers still on waivers, using the same projection as your lineup. Defenses show the gain over yours (or over the average defense), the chance of a big game (10 or more) and of a dud (0 or fewer) recomputed under your league's scoring from `js/dsthistory.js`, the opponent's expected score, and a dashed rough look-ahead of the next weeks. Kickers show a weather and roof chip carrying the model's own effect, and an honest note that streaming one is worth about a point. Rostered and taken players are left out; a league that starts neither position gets a plain message.
- **Collapsible alerts that stay as you leave them:** every alert is a collapsible box with a one-line headline that stays visible while it is closed (the lineup suggestion, the flex-order note, What to know on the game page, Worth knowing on the player page, the data-feed error, the early-season notice on Teams, and the Week screen's Things to know). A box you have never touched starts closed, except a data-feed error, which starts open. The open or closed choice is saved on the device per kind of alert (not per game or player) in `sharpline.folds`, so it survives navigation, refreshes and restarts; Lineup by source, the sources bar and the other older boxes remember too. A box the app opens by itself is never saved as if you had chosen it, and blocked storage only means the choice lasts for the session. Section headings stay real headings inside the summary.
- **Sub-menus:** every segmented bar is full width, so stacked bars share the same edges at any screen size, and a segment can never be narrower than its own label (the selected pill always encloses its text). Screens down to 320 px wide fit without sideways scrolling.
- **Range:** the likely range is not shown on list rows (how wide it is follows almost entirely from the projection). On the player page it is a "most weeks" bracket drawn under the dotplot's axis, spanning the middle 80% of the dots, so the words sit on the data. The hero is just the projected number.
- **Slate:** a ranked game list first, with the scatter map one tap away.

Colors and sizes live in `public/styles.css`. Team colors are in `public/js/teams.js`. The red-to-green scale is `chanceColor` in `public/js/charts.js`.

## Defense and kicker profiles

Rank 1 is always best for the defense (most sacks and takeaways, fewest yards allowed). Both pages follow the same order as other profiles: this week, season so far, where it stands, recent games, schedule ahead.
- **Defense**: The matchup (three tug bars, our strength against the opposing offense's weakness, with real ranks; it describes, it does not predict), How this defense has played, Where this defense stands (eight categories on one rank track, fantasy points under the user's own scoring, tap a row for its games), Who scores on them, Recent games (last five as bars linked to the full log), Schedule ahead.
- **Kicker**: This week: conditions (roof, wind, temperature and expected points from the app's kicker model), By the numbers (ranked among kickers), Kick map (every kick by distance and game; tap a distance to compare with the league), How his team scores (touchdowns against kicks), His offense, Schedule ahead. Percentages always sit next to made-of-tried because a season is 15 to 40 kicks.

A player who is not playing this week (bye, injured Out, no team, no line) keeps his season sections; only the sections about this week (big-game odds, how the number was built, the matchup, experts, conditions) are skipped, and a bye header names his next game.

Their game-by-game numbers come from the same nflverse weekly file the player model reads, parsed in the same pass and shipped inside the team table (defenses) and the usage data (kickers), so opening these pages makes no extra request. If the team table cannot load, the page says so, retries by itself three times and offers Try again. Not in the source: special-teams fumble recoveries and return yardage.

## Position tabs follow the league

The Players and Moves screens show a position tab only if some starting slot in the active league can use it. A league with no kicker or defense slot never sees K or DST, and those players also drop out of the All list. A Flex tab appears when any slot takes more than one position (Flex, W/R, W/T or Superflex) and lists RB, WR and TE. If the tab you were on stops applying after you switch leagues, the screen falls back to All. The rule lives in one place, `positionsFor` in `public/js/scoring.js`.

## How fresh the data is

Everyone sees the same data, because it is cached on the server and not on each phone. Each source has its own window:

| Data | Refreshed | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Betting lines, scores, game status (ESPN) | Live on every build of the feed | Netlify's CDN reuses one build for 60 seconds across all users, then serves it while rebuilding |
| Expert rankings (FantasyPros via dynastyprocess) | Server copy up to 45 minutes old | The source itself publishes about once a day |
| Sleeper projections, injury reports | Up to 30 minutes | |
| Game logs and opponent ratings | Up to 1 hour | |
| Forecast weather | Up to 3 hours per team and day | |
| Player list, schedule | 20 hours, 12 hours | Sleeper asks for the player list once a day |
| League rosters (Sleeper) | Checked when you open Moves, Players or Week if the saved copy is over 5 minutes old, every 10 minutes while the app stays open, and on demand | The server keeps a copy for 2 minutes; a manual Refresh skips it |

A phone checks again on its own: every minute while a game is live, every 3 minutes from 90 minutes before kickoff to 4 hours after, every 10 minutes otherwise, and whenever you come back to the app. It waits if you are typing or dragging, and it redraws only when a line, score, status or ranking actually changed (`public/js/refresh.js`). The refresh button still forces a check. The capture job (see above) also keeps the server copy warm during game windows.

## League rosters from Sleeper

Who is on each team decides who shows as a free agent, so it has to be current. Rosters are not on a calendar, because adds, drops, trades and waiver results can happen at any time; they are fetched when they are used.

- Opening Moves, Players or Week checks Sleeper if the saved rosters are over 5 minutes old. An app left open re-checks every 10 minutes. After a failure it waits a minute before trying again.
- Moves (and Players, when filtered to free agents or your team) shows "Rosters from Sleeper, checked 3 min ago" with a Refresh link. Leagues has a Refresh rosters button for each Sleeper league. A manual refresh asks the server to skip its 2-minute copy (`fresh=1`).
- A roster is the active list plus injured reserve plus the taxi squad, which Sleeper keeps in separate lists.
- For a synced league Sleeper is the source of truth, so players marked "taken" by hand are cleared on each sync. Manual leagues never call Sleeper.
- If Sleeper cannot be reached, or answers with nothing usable, the saved rosters are kept and the screen says how old they are. A sync never wipes a roster.
- Limit: Sleeper's rosters call shows who is on a team, not who is still locked on waivers after being dropped, so a player dropped yesterday can show as available until waivers clear.

## Matching players to their game logs

The stat model needs a player's game logs, which come from nflverse and are keyed by nflverse's own player id (the `gsis` id). Sleeper's player table is where the app learns who is who, but Sleeper does not document a `gsis` id field, and a player without one used to get no stat-model number and no injury status (the final number then fell back to the experts alone and the row said "experts only").

- The link now combines Sleeper's own id (which wins if it disagrees), the id table the app already downloads for expert rankings (it links 97 to 99 percent of this season's players, one to one), and, for anyone neither covers, a match on the same name and position when exactly one Sleeper player fits. If two players share a name and position nobody is guessed.
- Injury reports use the same link.
- Coverage is visible. `/api/diag` has a "Stat model" line (how many of this season's players are linked, and who is not). The Proof screen shows the same count. A player's own page says why the model has no number: not matched to game logs, fewer than 3 games logged, or game logs not loaded.
- The blend itself is mostly experts by design, because testing over five seasons found experts more accurate than a stats-only model: our model carries 28 percent for QBs, 29 for RBs, 17 for WRs and 36 for TEs. So the Blended order tracks expert rank closely. Use the "Our model" sort for the stat model's own ranking and "We like more" for where it disagrees with the experts.

## Privacy and accounts

- **Signing in and creating an account:** the invite code box is always visible (it is only checked when creating an account), what you typed is kept after a mistake (a wrong PIN clears just the PIN), the cursor lands in the field that needs fixing, the button says "Creating account…" or "Signing in…" while the server works, and the form does not depend on the browser reporting which button was tapped, which older phones do not. A request that takes over 25 seconds stops with a message.
- Each person's leagues, rosters and settings are stored under their own username. `/api/profile` returns only the signed-in user's own profile, answers nothing without a valid session token, and is never cached on the network or the device.
- Sleeper data is public on Sleeper itself: anyone who knows a league ID or Sleeper username can read its rosters there. The app adds no extra exposure, and its Sleeper lookup (`/api/sleeper`) now requires a signed-in user.
- PINs are salted and hashed (PBKDF2). Five wrong PINs lock that username for 15 minutes. Use a PIN of 6 or more digits.
- The person who owns the Netlify account can read everything stored in Blobs, including profiles.
- Endpoints that hold no personal data and need no sign-in: `feed`, `usage`, `players`, `accuracy`, `capture`, `diag`.

## Running record

`snapshot-scheduled` runs every 3 hours. For each player it saves the inputs the model had **before** that player's team kicked off, and freezes them at kickoff. `/api/accuracy` grades those frozen rows against actual PPR points for four sources (blend, our model, experts, Sleeper) and reports error, bias and range coverage. After two graded weeks (at least 200 player pairs) it tunes how much Sleeper counts inside the consensus (starting guess: 35%). Results appear on the Proof screen.

## Deploy

1. Push to a Git repo and connect it to Netlify, or use the Netlify CLI. No build step is needed (`publish = public`, functions in `netlify/functions`).
2. Optional environment variable: `INVITE_CODE` (if set, new accounts must enter it).
3. Netlify Blobs (accounts, profiles, frozen snapshots) needs no setup on Netlify.
4. Local development: `npx netlify dev`. Without Blobs configured, storage falls back to memory.

### Netlify free-plan budget

The free plan is a monthly pool of credits, and going over it pauses the site until the month resets. Production deploys, function compute and bandwidth all draw from it. This app is light (a scheduled function every 3 hours, small JSON responses), but deploys are the easy way to burn credits: batch your changes into a few deploys instead of pushing after every edit. Check Netlify's current pricing page for exact figures; they change.

## Known limits

- ESPN, Sleeper projections and Open-Meteo were not reachable from the environment this was built in, so those live calls were tested against recorded shapes. If one fails in production, the pulse shows it as waiting and the app keeps working without it.
- FantasyPros publishes Tuesday or Wednesday. Until then a new week runs on the stat model and Sleeper, and the app says so.
- Forecast wind is at 10 m and approximate, not a reading from the field.
- Kicker and defense numbers are estimates from the game line (kickers also use the spread and the weather), fitted to 2021 to 2025 results and checked on held-out seasons; they are not graded week by week, and a single week is mostly luck.
- The capture job depends on Netlify running a 15-minute schedule and on its 30-second limit for scheduled functions. A run that cannot finish says so in the function log and the next tick retries. Check Logs, then Functions, then capture-scheduled after the first deploy.
- Netlify Blobs storage limits were not confirmed. A week of captured history is roughly 25 KB in testing, but real weeks will differ.
- Snap counts are not used. The Sleeper share inside the consensus is an untested starting value until enough weeks are graded.
- Injury news after the last refresh can change everything; check status near kickoff.
